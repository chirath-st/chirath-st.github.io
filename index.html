Case 01Agoda, BangkokCommercial Operations and AI Automation InternJun–Aug 2026

Possible sales opportunities, flagged automatically, approved by a person.

BeforeThe team ran its sales checks by hand.→AfterThe checks run on their own. A person approves each opportunity.

1 Situation

The commercial team ran its sales checks by hand. One check looked for hotels and suppliers selling on Agoda but not yet switched on with partner platforms such as Booking.com and Priceline.

My manager wanted the checks to run on their own, with the results shown for review.

2 What I did

  • Mapped the process with the business owner, my manager: find the gap, apply the rules, review, write the note.
  • Asked account managers what a useful note must say and what they should do next.
  • Built the team’s market and invoicing rules into the checks, so gaps that are there on purpose are filtered out before anyone reviews them.
  • Reviewed results with my manager at least twice a week and logged the issues we found.

3 Result

Live

A person still ticks each opportunity before a draft note is prepared for the account manager. I handed it over with a week spent teaching my manager how it works.

So whatThe aim: the team spends its time on the calls worth making, not on finding them.

Case 02Agoda, BangkokCommercial Operations and AI Automation InternJun–Aug 2026

One place for a team’s questions, each sent to the right lane.

BeforeQuestions came in by email, Slack and Teams.→AfterOne channel. Three bots answer, look it up, or pass it to a person.

1 Situation

Colleagues brought the team questions by email, Slack and Teams. Some could take several rounds of back-and-forth before anyone could start on them.

2 What I did

  • Worked with my manager, who set the requirements, on one channel for all of it.
  • Interviewed each team member about their role and the kinds of cases they should get, and mapped which question should reach which person.
  • Worked through four versions: an existing platform, a custom bot, then, on the internal platform team’s advice to reuse existing bots, one existing bot, and finally three existing bots working together.
  • Final design: one bot answers common questions, one looks things up in company sources, one tags the right people and opens a ticket with the findings attached.

3 Result

Live

Live, like Case 01.

So whatThe aim: questions arrive in one place and reach the right person with the context already attached.

Case 03BuzzeBees, BangkokData Scientist InternJun–Aug 2025

Helping an overseas AI client decide which data it could use.

BeforeAn open question: which of the company’s data could help?→AfterData sorted into three groups. The client agreed on data for selected products.

1 Situation

Omnisient, an AI and data company from South Africa, wanted to know which of the company’s data could help train its models.

The talks were in English, and I was allowed to speak with the client directly while working with the product and data teams.

2 What I did

  • Spoke directly with the client about what they needed.
  • Asked the product team what data existed, how much and how it was stored, and worked with a senior data scientist to pull what was relevant.
  • Helped the team sort the data into three groups: offer now, needs more information or a partner’s OK, not suitable.

3 Result

Client agreed on data for selected products

My manager told me my part helped the talks finish sooner.

I also built an early model to predict whether paying to boost a partner’s post pays back, and handed the prototype to a senior colleague to develop further.

So whatThe aim: turn an open question into clear choices the client could decide on.

Case 04Rutgers Road to Silicon ValleyTeam of three2025

Cheaper rides between campuses: a campus ride-pooling pitch.

BeforeWhat we saw: crowded, slow buses between campuses.→AfterA ride-pooling idea, shown as a clickable prototype.

1 Situation

What we saw: buses between the Rutgers–New Brunswick campuses were crowded, slow and hard to track.

Our team’s first idea was a summer-sublet app, and I doubted summer supply and demand would match.

2 What I did

  • Proposed switching to campus ride-pooling.
  • Designed how it works: students already driving to class post their seats, and riders join and pay the driver.
  • Used our team’s student survey on interest, willingness to pay and Uber spending to make the case on price.
  • Co-presented the pitch with a teammate.

3 Result

Pitch · clickable prototype

Top 20 finalist at Rutgers Road to Silicon Valley. We showed the judges a clickable Figma prototype.

So whatThe idea: seats in cars already heading to class become the supply, which keeps the price low.
