Questions in one channel, answered or routed

Colleagues' questions used to arrive by email, Slack and Teams, and each one needed back-and-forth before anyone could help. Now one Slack channel reads every question, answers the common ones, looks things up when needed, and opens a ticket for a person when it has to.

01 · the problem

Three inboxes, one small team

Every question landed wherever the asker happened to be.

Day-to-day operational questions reached the team as an email, a Slack message or a Teams chat. Whoever saw it first had to ask for the missing details, wait, ask again, and only then start investigating.

The same kind of question came back again and again, and each time somebody typed the answer again. When a case needed a ticket, the context was copied over by hand.

The team wanted one place to ask, where a question gets dealt with.

02 · what I built

One channel, three helpers, three outcomes

Post the question once. The channel sorts it.

A reader bot reads every message in the channel, no tagging needed. If it is a known question, it answers straight away from an prepared answers the team keeps for recurring issues.

If the question needs looking up, it calls a second bot that searches internal documents and writes a clear reply. If a person has to step in, a third bot opens a Jira ticket with the original message, what was found and a suggested next step, and tags the right teammate.

03 · what a person still decides

The bots fetch and type. People judge.

No bot closes a case on its own. When a question needs real work — a fix, a decision, a call to someone — a ticket lands with a teammate who investigates and resolves it. I prepared the first set of answers; the team can add an agreed answer once, and the first bot reuses it instead of a person retyping it.

05 · how I built it

Listen, map, try, wire, fill

Listened first

I interviewed every team member: what do you handle, which cases should reach you? Their answers became the routing rules.

Mapped the flow

With my manager I turned the team's needs into a workflow map: when to answer, when to look something up, when to ask the asker for more, when to open a ticket.

Tried four versions

Each version was shaped by what the one before could not do. The fourth — three existing bots working together — is the one in use.

Wired the three

I wrote the behaviour rules: the reader bot reads without being tagged and decides which helper to call; the research bot answers in a clean format; the ticket bot connects to the team's Jira.

Filled the prepared answers

Prepared answers for recurring issues — what is known, whether a fix exists, what to do next — so the first bot can reuse them instead of a person retyping.
