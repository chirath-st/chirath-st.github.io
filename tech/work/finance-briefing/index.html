The morning news, read to me.

Every weekday a small pipeline collects the news, picks what matters, writes a short script with an AI model, turns it into a natural voice, and sends the audio, a recap, photo cards and links to my Telegram. Built for one listener: me.

The problem

Too many tabs before breakfast.

I wanted to keep up with markets, tech and world news without scrolling several sites every morning.

And I didn't want to make the usual low-effort AI content. If a machine was going to summarise the news for me, it had to be good enough to actually listen to, every day.

the old way
finance site · newspaper · tech feed · …every morning
The old way: several sites, a pile of articles, read it all or miss it. Every morning.

How I built it · five plain steps
01
A timer starts itWeekdays, around 11 am New York time: after the opening bell, so there are articles to summarise. Nothing to press.
02
It collects the newsSmall Python scripts pull stories from several finance-news services and major newspapers, plus the day’s market moves, and skip repeats.
03
An AI model picks and writesIt ranks what actually moved markets, ties the moves to their causes, and writes a short script in a fixed shape — a global edition and one filtered to what I follow.
04
A voice reads itText-to-speech turns the script into audio. I tested voices first; this one takes notes on tone, so it doesn’t sound like a robot.
05
Telegram gets the packageThe audio is stored online, the bot posts the recap, audio, photo cards and link list, and a tiny status file is updated for this site.
I decide what to read in full.

for the technical reader

scheduler = a program that runs a task at set times; here n8n · feed = the machine-readable version of a news site · Claude API = the AI model, called by code with my instructions and rules · Inworld TTS = the voice service, Google’s voice as the backup · Cloudflare R2 = file storage; it also feeds a private podcast app.

What a person still decides

Nothing is reviewed before it sends. That’s the point.

the green diamond

It’s my own briefing, so no one signs off on each message. What I decide is what to open (every story links back to the original), what stays in (I tweak the format run after run; the first version is never right), and what stays out. Generated charts looked convincing but their numbers weren’t reliable, so I dropped the generated charts. Video stayed an experiment.

What it removed

Three things I stopped doing by hand.

Repetition
was: open several sites, every morning

Summaries and the links now arrive in one place.

Hand-starting
was: remember to go and look

Nothing to trigger. It runs itself on weekdays and tells me if a step failed.

Reading
was: read everything to find the few that matter

Listen instead, a few minutes a day. Open a link only when I want more.

A personal tool that runs itself each weekday.
Honest split

My part, and not my part.

My part

  • The idea and the bar: useful every day, not AI slop.
  • Choosing the sources and what counts as important.
  • Designing the pipeline and wiring the pieces together.
  • Picking the voice after testing options, and tuning its tone.
  • Testing image tools (cinematic vs cartoon) and dropping charts when their numbers weren't right.
  • Tweaking the output, run after run. Adding fallbacks and the status file.

Not my part

  • The journalism. Every item links to the original article and names its source.
  • The summaries and the narration. Claude and Inworld do those, inside rules I set.
  • The timing. A scheduler starts it; I don't press anything.
  • AI helped me research each tool before I built with it, and dig further when it wasn’t sure.
