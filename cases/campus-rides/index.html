What we saw

Buses between campuses were crowded, slow and hard to track.

Rutgers–New Brunswick students travel between campuses to get to class.

On those trips we saw crowded buses, traffic, and buses that were hard to track. Long trips between campuses got in the way of class schedules.

That became the problem our pitch set out to solve.

The pivot

I questioned our first idea and proposed campus ride-pooling instead.

A friend invited me to Rutgers Road to Silicon Valley, a pitching competition. Our team of three first looked at a swipe app for summer sublets, for students leaving their apartments.

I doubted that summer supply and demand would match, and proposed a different idea: campus ride-pooling.

It matches students who need a ride between campuses with students already driving to class.

How a ride works

Students already driving to class post their trip. Riders join and pay the driver.

The drivers are making the trip anyway. Their empty seats become rides between the campuses: College Avenue, Busch, Livingston and Cook/Douglass.

What I did

I designed how the service works, for drivers, riders and the app.

  1. Questioned our first idea and proposed campus ride-pooling instead.
  2. Designed how it works: students already driving to class post the route, departure time and price; riders join a trip and pay the driver; the app keeps a commission.
  3. Used our team’s student survey on interest, willingness to pay and Uber spending to make the case on price.
  4. Co-presented the pitch with a teammate.

The price argument

Students told us what they spend on Uber. We used it to make our case on price.

Our team ran a student survey for the pitch. It asked about three things:

  • Interest in using the service
  • Willingness to pay
  • Current Uber spending

We used what students told us about Uber to argue that a seat in a car already going to class could cost much less than an Uber.

What we showed the judges

A clickable prototype of the app, so the judges could see how it works.

It showed demo cars on a campus map, the same rides as a list by campus, a page of past rides, and settings with a dark mode.

What happened

We pitched Dip, our campus ride-pooling idea, at Rutgers Road to Silicon Valley.
