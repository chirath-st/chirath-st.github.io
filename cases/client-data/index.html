The situation

An overseas AI client asked which of the company’s data could help train its models.

BuzzeBees is a loyalty-service provider in Bangkok. Omnisient, an AI and data company from South Africa, visited to discuss which of the company’s data could be useful for training AI models.

The talks were in English, and I was allowed to speak with the client directly.

The question on the table

Which of the company’s data could help train the client’s models?

Who I worked with

I spoke with the client, and brought in the product and data teams.

I talked through the client’s data needs with them directly. My English and my recent machine-learning work helped me follow the technical side.

Inside the company, I took the client’s questions to the product team and to a senior data scientist, and brought the answers back to the talks.

What I did

I found out what data existed, how much, how it was stored and how it could fit.

  1. Asked the product team what data the company had.
  2. Found out roughly how much there was and how it was stored.
  3. Looked at how it might relate to what the client wanted to use it for.
  4. Worked with a senior data scientist to pull the relevant data for the talks.

Three groups

We sorted the data into three groups the client could decide on.

Some data could be offered right away. Some needed more information or a partner’s OK first. Some was not suitable, or unlikely to be approved.

What the client decided

The client agreed on data for selected products.

  • Which data to use. The client chose from three clear groups and agreed on data for selected products.

The team also showed the client the expected size and structure of the data, as rows and columns.

Also at BuzzeBees

I built an early prototype to predict whether boosting a post pays back.

Partner Facebook pages post products with an affiliate link. The company pays to boost a post and earns a commission when people buy. The question: how much to spend on a boost for the best return? I handed the prototype to a senior colleague to develop further.

What I built

I linked the money spent on boosting posts to the commissions they earned.

  1. Wrote SQL queries that link Facebook ad spend, impressions and clicks with commissions and orders from Shopee and Lazada.
  2. Matched the two sides through shared tracking IDs, so spend and commission sit side by side.
  3. Worked out the return measures: click-through rate, cost per click, return on ad spend and return on investment.
  4. Built an early model to predict the return, and handed it to a senior colleague as a prototype to develop further.

What happened

The client agreed on data, and my prototype went to a senior colleague.

Client agreed on data

The client agreed on data for selected products. My manager told me my part helped the talks finish sooner.

I handed the ad-return prototype to a senior colleague to develop further.
