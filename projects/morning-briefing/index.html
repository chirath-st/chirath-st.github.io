The 60-second version

What I built
A small automation that collects the day’s news, has an AI model pick what matters and write a short script, and has a natural-sounding voice read it. The audio, a recap, photo cards and the links land in my Telegram.
Who did what
The idea, the quality bar and the design were mine; AI helped me research each tool first. AI models write the summaries and read them aloud, inside rules I set. Every story links back to the original article.
Runs as
A timer, every weekday around 11 am New York time: after the opening bell, so there are articles to summarise. Nothing to press.

01/ 04

Part 1 of 4: The problem

I wanted the day’s news without opening several sites every morning.

Markets, tech and world news in one place. If a machine was going to summarise it for me, it had to be good enough to listen to every day.

02/ 04

Part 2 of 4: What I built

One message each weekday: press play, or read the recap.

Around 11 am New York time, after the opening bell, it runs on its own and sends the package to my Telegram.

Part 3 of 4: My choices

I tested each part before it went into the daily message.

I listened to different voices, compared image styles and tried AI-made charts. Then I kept tweaking the format, run after run.

The bar I setWorth listening to every day.

My part, and what the AI does2 short lists · under 1 min

My part

  • The idea and the bar: worth listening to every day.
  • Designing the steps and wiring the tools together.
  • Choosing the voice after testing options, and tuning its tone.
  • Tweaking the format, run after run.

What the AI and the timer do

  • AI models write the summaries and read them aloud, inside rules I set.
  • AI helped me research each tool before I built with it.
  • A timer starts every run; I don’t press anything.
  • The journalism is the newsrooms’: every item names its source and links to it.
