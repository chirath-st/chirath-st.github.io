The 60-second version

What I did
Rebuilt a published model that predicts short-term price moves from the order book, then tested it against six others, from simple statistics to variations on its design: 95 experiments, written up in a research report.
Who did what
The club set the brief. I took it further on my own, with AI assistants helping me explore the research, write the code and draft the report.
Tools
Python and PyTorch. I asked for access to the Rutgers Amarel GPU cluster; each run was a settings file, queued there and saved with its results.

01/ 04

Part 1 of 4: The question

The brief was to rebuild one published model. I asked whether all of it was needed.

The model, DeepLOB, reads the order book, the list of open offers to buy and sell a stock, and predicts whether the price goes up, down or stays flat.

What I didKept the published model as the yardstick and tested six others beside it.

The data, in plain wordsKey · under 1 min
The stocks
Five Finnish stocks over ten trading days, from a public benchmark (FI-2010).
One example
The last 100 order-book updates, ten price levels on each side.
The question
Up, down or flat, 10, 20, 30, 50 or 100 updates ahead.
Learn, then test
Models learn on the first seven days and are scored on the last three.

Part 2 of 4: What I built

I built a test bench: every model gets the same data and the same question.

95 experimentsSeven models, five horizons, four ways to train for trading.The published model, rebuilt, is one row. The other six range from simple statistics to variations on its design.

03/ 04

Part 3 of 4: Trading costs

Then I charged every simulated trade its cost.

A second scorecard turns each model’s predictions into simple trades, and each change of position pays half the bid–ask spread. On that scorecard, a higher accuracy score did not reliably mean better simulated trading.

What I didTested ways to trade less: holding longer, acting only when several horizons agree, sitting out volatile moments.

What the bid–ask spread is, in one picture1 exhibit · under 1 min

Why each change costs

The price is tracked in the middle, between the best offer to buy (the bid) and the best offer to sell (the ask). A real trade happens at the bid or the ask, half a spread away from the middle.

04/ 04

Part 4 of 4: What I found

Submitted to the Rutgers Quant Finance Club

A model 3.8 times smaller scored about the same.

On one public dataset, averaged over the five horizons. My takeaway: a more complicated model is not necessarily a better one.
