The 60-second version

What I built
A planner for my own day. Each morning it posts one plan in Slack: classes, coursework, audio versions of my readings and anything I add by hand. Later in the day it re-sorts the plan and follows up on what is left.
Who did what
I designed it: what it covers, the daily routine, and what to change after using it. An AI assistant wrote the code and set up the connections.
Runs as
Scheduled runs on my own computer: the plan each morning, a re-sort later in the day. Every change is kept in a spreadsheet.

01/ 04

Part 1 of 4: Why I built it

I wanted school, career and personal tasks in one place.

It began as a way to find course slides I was missing. I turned it into a planner for my whole day.

02/ 04

Part 2 of 4: The morning plan

Each morning, one Slack post lays out my day.

Grouped so it reads at a glanceClasses, coursework, audio, my own tasks.Coursework is grouped by course, and each reading comes with an audio version.

The reading library: each reading sent once1 exhibit · under 1 min

Audio versions of my readings

Each course reading is kept in a small library and sent to Slack as audio. If the same reading turns up again, it is not sent twice.

03/ 04

Part 3 of 4: When the day changes

When the day changes, the plan re-sorts itself.

It runs again later in the day, puts anything new in its place, and follows up on what is not done yet.

What I decidedThe daily routine: a plan in the morning, then a re-sort and a follow-up as the day goes on.

Every change, kept in a spreadsheet1 exhibit · under 1 min

A log I can check

Each change is added as a new line, never written over, so I can see what changed and when. The log is kept on my computer and in Google Sheets.

04/ 04

Part 4 of 4: What happened

It still plans my day. I shaped it by using it and asking for changes.

Since September 2026 it posts a plan each morning, re-sorts later in the day and follows up on what is left.
